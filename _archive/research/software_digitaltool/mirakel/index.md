# MIRAKEL — Mixed Reality and AI for urban mining and reuse assessment

## Research positioning

**MIRAKEL** is a research project developing a mixed-reality and trustworthy-AI-based assistance system for assessing anthropogenic material stocks and resource-recovery costs in the circular economy. Its reuse relevance lies in the early assessment of existing buildings before deconstruction, especially where documentation is incomplete or difficult to interpret.

MIRAKEL is not mainly a marketplace or design tool. It is better understood as a digital assistance system for **urban mining**, pre-deconstruction assessment, and decision support about reuse and recycling potential.

## Main reuse problem addressed

Many buildings contain reusable components and valuable materials, but owners and planners often lack reliable data about what is inside the building, where it is located, in what quantity, and whether it can be recovered economically. This makes reuse decisions late, uncertain, and expensive.

MIRAKEL addresses this by improving data availability, standardising pre-deconstruction assessments, and supporting early estimation of reuse and recycling potential, costs, and environmental impacts.

## How it supports reuse

MIRAKEL supports reuse by helping experts assess existing buildings as material stocks. Its AI and mixed-reality approach can support:

- on-site identification of components and materials;
- structured capture of building-stock data;
- improved pre-deconstruction assessment;
- estimation of reuse and recycling potential;
- early cost and environmental-impact evaluation;
- better decision-making before demolition or renovation;
- communication of hidden material value to owners and project teams.

## Typical data inputs

Potential inputs include:

- building geometry and spatial information;
- drawings, archive documents, and plans;
- on-site observations;
- photos or scans;
- material and component classifications;
- expert annotations;
- cost assumptions for dismantling and recovery;
- environmental-impact assumptions;
- regulatory and quality-assurance information.

## Typical outputs

Likely outputs include:

- digital assessment of anthropogenic stock value;
- reuse and recycling potential estimates;
- recovery-cost estimates;
- environmental-impact indicators;
- structured pre-deconstruction assessment data;
- visual or mixed-reality guidance for experts on site;
- decision-support information for owners, planners, and deconstruction teams.

## Place in a reuse workflow

MIRAKEL is most relevant at the beginning of a reuse process:

1. identify a building or portfolio with potential for urban mining;
2. collect existing documents and on-site information;
3. use mixed reality and AI assistance to capture and interpret material-stock data;
4. estimate reuse and recycling potential;
5. compare recovery costs and environmental benefits;
6. decide whether to pursue selective deconstruction, component reuse, recycling, or continued use.

## Relevance to circular construction research

MIRAKEL is important because it addresses the data bottleneck in urban mining. Reuse often depends on knowing the building stock before the demolition decision is final. By combining AI, mixed reality, and standardised assessment, the project explores how digital tools can make hidden material stocks more visible and actionable.

It is especially relevant for research on:

- AI-supported urban mining;
- mixed-reality assistance for building surveys;
- pre-deconstruction audit digitalisation;
- anthropogenic stock assessment;
- trustworthy AI in construction decision-making;
- cost and environmental assessment of reuse pathways;
- data capture for circular deconstruction.

## Strengths for reuse

- Focuses on the early phase where many reuse opportunities are lost.
- Supports systematic assessment rather than informal salvage.
- Helps estimate both technical potential and economic feasibility.
- Can improve transparency about hidden material stocks.
- Combines expert work with digital assistance rather than replacing professional judgement.

## Limitations and research gaps

- It is a research and prototype-oriented system, not necessarily a mature commercial platform.
- AI results depend on data quality, model reliability, and expert validation.
- Material identification does not automatically prove reusability; testing and approval remain necessary.
- Mixed-reality workflows must be usable on real construction sites with time and safety constraints.
- Economic feasibility depends on market demand, logistics, labour costs, and regulation.
