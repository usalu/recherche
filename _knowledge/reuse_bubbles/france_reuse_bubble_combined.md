# France / Île-de-France Reuse Bubble — combined research + evidence file

_Last updated: 2026-06-05_

## How to read this file

This file combines two layers that were previously separated:

1. **Knowledge / interpretation** — the network map, actor roles, material-flow logic, and why the French ecosystem can be read as an infrastructural reuse “bubble”.
2. **Evidence** — footnotes, source notes, confidence levels, and a claim-by-claim ledger.

The focus is **reclaimed building-material reuse**: components, products, equipment, and materials that can be reused with minimal or controlled transformation. Where a source mixes _réemploi_, _réutilisation_, _recyclage_, _valorisation_, bio-based materials, or eco-design, this file separates those terms as carefully as possible.

## 0. Executive thesis

France — especially **Île-de-France**, with important regional extensions to **Lyon/Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes**, **Toulouse/Occitanie**, **Marseille/Southern France**, **Lorient/Brittany**, and **Hauts-de-France** — has one of the clearest European reuse ecosystems because it contains all the layers needed for reuse to become infrastructural:

- **regulation and traceability** through the PEMD diagnostic and REP PMCB system;[^pemd][^ademe-pmcb]
- **research and standardization** through ADEME, CSTB, SPIROU, REPAR, FCRBE and PREUSE;[^ademe-reemploi][^cstb-reemploi][^ademe-spirou][^fcrbe][^preuse]
- **market visibility** through Cycle Up, Backacia, Opalis, RAEDIFICARE, Minéka, Mobius and other platforms or depots;[^cycleup][^backacia][^opalis][^raedificare][^mineka][^mobius]
- **demand aggregation** through Booster du Réemploi / A4MT and public clients;[^booster][^a4mt][^life-toulouse]
- **technical trust and insurance work** through CSTB/SPIROU, Qualiconsult, Mobius, Cycle Up and FCRBE insurance resources;[^cstb-reemploi][^ademe-spirou][^fcrbe-insurance]
- **physical reconditioning and redistribution** through workshops, reuse centres and local platforms such as Cycle Up Noisy-le-Sec, Cycle Up/Eiffage/Demcy/ITEM in Rillieux-la-Pape, Mobius Bondy, Minéka, RéaVie, and RAEDIFICARE;[^cycleup-idf][^cycleup-eiffage][^mobius-idf][^mineka][^reavie][^raedificare]
- **built demonstrators** such as Actlab, La Fabrique du Clos, Maison des Canaux, Pavillon Circulaire, Pavillon Keller and LIFE Waste2Build pilot operations.[^actlab][^fabrique-clos][^maison-canaux][^pavillon-circulaire][^pavillon-keller][^life-envirobat]

**Interpretive conclusion:** the French bubble is not as tightly centered as the Swiss Cirkla / Zirkular / baubüro in situ cluster. It is more **polycentric**: Paris/Île-de-France provides the densest institutional and real-estate ecosystem; Toulouse shows public procurement as a reuse lever; Lyon shows local platforms and reconditioning; Southern France shows RAEDIFICARE’s sector-building role; Northwestern Europe links France to Rotor, Bellastock, Opalis, FCRBE and PREUSE.

## 1. Working definition: what counts as “infrastructural reuse”?

For this research, a reuse initiative is considered **infrastructural** when it helps future projects reuse materials more easily. A single beautiful building with reclaimed materials is not automatically infrastructural. It becomes infrastructural when it contributes to one or more repeatable conditions:

| Layer | What it means | French examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Resource identification** | identifying, auditing and characterizing materials before demolition or renovation | PEMD diagnostic, diagnostic ressources, Cycle Up, Bellastock, Backacia, Mobius |
| **Market visibility** | making supply/demand searchable and comparable | Cycle Up, Backacia, Opalis, RAEDIFICARE platform, Looping |
| **Physical logistics** | storing, cleaning, preparing, reconditioning, transporting | Cycle Up Noisy-le-Sec, Mobius Bondy, Minéka, RéaVie, RAEDIFICARE, reconditioning workshops |
| **Technical trust** | proving performance, safety, condition and insurability | CSTB, SPIROU, Qualiconsult, ADEME, FCRBE insurance resources |
| **Demand aggregation** | getting clients and buyers to specify reuse systematically | Booster du Réemploi, A4MT, Toulouse Métropole/LIFE Waste2Build, public procurement |
| **Knowledge transfer** | guides, material sheets, training, case studies, professional resources | Bellastock, REPAR, FCRBE, PREUSE, Opalis, Ekopolis, ADEME, Valobat |
| **Demonstrators** | built projects that prove methods in real contexts | Actlab, Fabrique du Clos, Maison des Canaux, Pavillon Circulaire, Pavillon Keller |

## 3. Why France is a strong reuse bubble

### 3.1 Regulation creates a minimum visibility layer

The **diagnostic PEMD** is central. The French Ministry states that the PEMD diagnostic applies to certain demolition or significant renovation operations — including operations where the cumulative floor area exceeds 1,000 m² — and that it provides information about products, equipment, materials and waste expected from the operation, with reuse prioritized before recovery.[^pemd]

This does not automatically create reuse, but it creates a **formal moment of visibility** before demolition. In infrastructural terms, it means the building is no longer only a future waste stream; it becomes a potential inventory of components.

### 3.2 REP PMCB creates a waste-management and circularity infrastructure

ADEME describes the REP PMCB system for construction products and materials. Its PMCB page notes collection patterns, including an estimate that around 80% of PMCB waste is collected directly from construction sites and around 20% via collection points, and describes the deployment of collection-point networks by eco-organizations.[^ademe-pmcb]

This is not the same as reuse. Much of REP PMCB concerns waste management, sorting, recycling and recovery. However, it forms a logistics and compliance backdrop into which reuse actors can plug. The French reuse bubble is therefore tied to a larger **waste-to-resource institutional system**.

### 3.3 Research has produced tools, not just discourse

ADEME’s study on construction-material reuse says it resulted from three years of work to remove barriers to reuse in France; outputs included documenting 40 professional resellers active in France, accompanying project owners on 10 test sites, and producing 10 reclaimed-material sheets.[^ademe-reemploi]

That is important because it shows France has not only isolated architecture projects, but a documented attempt to build **market knowledge** and **professional guidance**.

### 3.4 Public procurement is being used as a scaling lever

Toulouse Métropole states that LIFE Waste2Build uses public procurement to develop the use of second-life materials in construction, renovation and landscaping, beginning with listing available materials and classifying them according to possible reuse.[^life-toulouse] Envirobat Occitanie describes the project goals as reducing the BTP sector’s impact by 20%, structuring the circular BTP sector, implementing more circular purchasing policies, and supporting skills development.[^life-envirobat]

This is infrastructural because public procurement can create repeatable demand, not only isolated “pilot” demand.

## 4. Actor map: core nodes and their roles

### 4.1 Bellastock — practice, research, experimentation and culture

**Role in the bubble:** Bellastock is the main French actor for turning reuse into an architectural, territorial and pedagogical method.

Bellastock’s reuse page states that it masters each link in the reuse chain, from diagnosis of deposits and selective deconstruction to reuse in future constructions.[^bellastock-reemploi] ADEME describes Bellastock as a structure that has developed pioneering expertise in France on the reuse of construction materials since 2012.[^ademe-bellastock]

**Key contributions:**

- **Actlab** — a live laboratory, technical/cultural resource and platform of experimentation at Île-Saint-Denis; Bellastock calls it the first “laboratoire manifeste” for reuse of materials in France.[^actlab]
- **REPAR / REPAR #2** — research and expertise programme on reuse, with the ADEME page describing REPAR as a programme piloted by Bellastock in partnership with ADEME and CSTB since 2012.[^ademe-repar]
- **La Fabrique du Clos** — a chantier-recyclerie and territorial reuse prototype in Stains, linked to demolition, storage, prototypes, training, public space and future prescriptions.[^fabrique-clos]
- **PREUSE / Opalis** — Bellastock works with Rotor on Opalis and PREUSE, linking France to the Northwestern Europe reuse network.[^opalis-about][^preuse-bellastock]

**Interpretation:** Bellastock is not simply a design office. In this ecosystem it functions like a **method generator**: it makes reuse visible, teachable, contractable and demonstrable.

**Confidence:** high for projects and role; “method generator” is an interpretation supported by the range of activities.

---

### 4.2 Cycle Up — marketplace, services, diagnostics and reconditioning

**Role in the bubble:** Cycle Up is one of the clearest French market-infrastructure actors.

Cycle Up states that since 2018 it has developed tools and services for building and construction actors to accelerate reuse and circular economy; it describes a marketplace designed to match supply and demand and connect owners, project managers, architects, engineers, demolition contractors, reconditioners and builders.[^cycleup]

Cycle Up also describes consulting and support services including AMO/AMOE reuse, resource and PEMD diagnostics, territorial flow studies, design support, strategic advice and circular data.[^cycleup]

**Physical reconditioning layer:**

- The Île-de-France Region describes a project to create a Cycle Up reconditioning workshop in **Noisy-le-Sec** for reuse materials from Francilian construction sites, with machinery for reconditioning, upcycling and storage, and a projected capacity of 6,000 sanitary items per year.[^cycleup-idf]
- Eiffage states that Cycle Up, Eiffage Construction, Demcy and ITEM created a **360 m² workshop in Rillieux-la-Pape**, near Lyon, to recondition finishing materials from deconstruction sites, beginning with ceramic sanitary ware and cable trays.[^cycleup-eiffage]
- An FCRBE insurance case study says Cycle Up classifies listings by confidence level and that sanitary fittings arriving at Noisy-le-Sec are assessed, cleaned and inspected before resale/reuse.[^fcrbe-cycleup]

**Interpretation:** Cycle Up is a bridge between digital matching and physical material preparation. It is therefore stronger than a pure classified-ad marketplace.

**Confidence:** high for marketplace and workshop claims; medium for actual long-term quantities unless supported by annual operational data.

---

### 4.3 Booster du Réemploi / A4MT — demand-side infrastructure

**Role in the bubble:** Booster du Réemploi helps make reuse demand visible and collective.

Booster du Réemploi describes its mission as getting property stakeholders to join forces to recommend material reuse and to ramp up and consolidate reuse in the building sector.[^booster] A4MT describes the Reuse Booster as a collective action programme supporting contracting authorities, including public and private project sponsors and project engineers.[^a4mt]

The associated **Looping** platform is described by ADEME as a tool to make demand visible, stimulate supply and calculate carbon/environmental benefits.[^looping]

**Interpretation:** most reuse ecosystems fail because supply and demand do not meet at the right time, quality and quantity. Booster is therefore infrastructural not because it stores material, but because it attempts to create **predictable demand**.

**Confidence:** high for mission; medium for measured market effect.

---

### 4.4 CSTB / SPIROU / Qualiconsult / ADEME — technical trust and insurability

**Role in the bubble:** this is the technical and insurance layer.

CSTB states that reuse needs to be structured to recognize requalification processes and performance-justification methods for PEM — products, equipment and materials — and that the SPIROU project aims to harmonize and secure reuse practices so they can move more quickly toward insurance recognition.[^cstb-reemploi]

CSTB lists SPIROU partners as ADEME, CSTB, Mobius Réemploi, Booster du Réemploi / A4MT, and Qualiconsult.[^cstb-reemploi] ADEME’s SPIROU page says the project aims to develop shared operating procedures to secure reuse practices and support supply-side actors, including methodological diagnostic notes and characterization protocols for ten product families.[^ademe-spirou]

CSTB identifies ten families in the SPIROU work, including wooden door blocks/fire doors, ceramic sanitary appliances, carpet tiles, traditional timber frames, water radiators, electrical cabinets and modular protection, luminaires, lime-mortar bricks, mineral cladding, and rigid galvanized-steel ventilation ducts.[^cstb-reemploi]

**Interpretation:** SPIROU addresses one of the biggest barriers to material reuse: the question “can this component be accepted by insurers, control offices, contractors and clients?”

**Confidence:** high.

---

### 4.5 Opalis / FCRBE / PREUSE — European visibility and policy-scaling layer

**Role in the bubble:** Opalis and the FCRBE/PREUSE programmes connect France to the broader Northwestern European reuse infrastructure.

Opalis describes itself as a resource for building and renovating with reclaimed materials, including professional suppliers, common materials and project examples.[^opalis] ADEME describes Opalis as a platform whose goal is to facilitate the use of reclaimed materials in construction and renovation projects through a supplier directory, technical documentation, project examples and links.[^ademe-opalis]

Opalis says it was developed in FCRBE and is maintained from 2024 to 2027 by Rotor and Bellastock as part of PREUSE.[^opalis-about] Bellastock’s Opalis France page says that since summer 2021 it has worked with Rotor to develop Opalis in France and that in 2024 this work created 32 new sheets, conducted 19 visits and updated nearly 270 profiles.[^bellastock-opalis]

FCRBE aimed to increase by 50% the amount of reclaimed building elements circulating in its territory by 2032.[^fcrbe] PREUSE aims to help local and regional public authorities improve management of material resources and implement systemic solutions for reclaiming and reusing building materials.[^preuse]

**Interpretation:** Opalis is an information infrastructure. FCRBE and PREUSE are policy/knowledge infrastructures. Together they allow French actors to learn from, and contribute to, the Rotor/Belgium/Northwestern Europe reuse field.

**Confidence:** high.

---

### 4.6 Backacia — digital marketplace + service layer

**Role in the bubble:** Backacia is a private digital marketplace and service provider for reused construction materials and equipment.

Backacia describes itself as an online platform for construction professionals to buy and sell second-hand construction materials and equipment.[^backacia] Opalis lists Backacia as a company created in 2017 whose main activity is a digital marketplace focused on reuse of construction materials and elements; it also lists services such as reuse studies/AMO, curage, a material library, and storage/resale.[^opalis-backacia]

**Interpretation:** Backacia sits at the interface of audit, curage, storage, visibility and sale.

**Confidence:** high for actor description; medium for scale.

---

### 4.7 Mobius Réemploi — supply, reconditioning and technical viability

**Role in the bubble:** Mobius is a supply/reconditioning actor that also appears in technical-standardization work.

Mobius states that it develops reuse in the building sector by sourcing and supplying construction materials from reuse and ensuring technical viability.[^mobius] The Île-de-France Region describes a Mobius Réemploi project in Bondy involving new premises, equipment and machines to develop production of reconditioned raised-floor tiles, brick reconditioning and furniture upcycling.[^mobius-idf]

Mobius is also listed by CSTB as a SPIROU partner.[^cstb-reemploi]

**Interpretation:** Mobius is important because it links the supply chain to the technical trust layer: sourcing, preparation, traceability, and insurability.

**Confidence:** high for role; medium for actual production volume.

---

### 4.8 Minéka — local and solidarity-based platform in Lyon/Villeurbanne

**Role in the bubble:** Minéka represents a local, social-solidarity reuse model.

Minéka states that its mission is to develop material reuse in construction and that since 2016 it has carried a local and solidarity-based vision of reuse to reduce BTP waste.[^mineka] France Num describes Minéka as a Villeurbanne association founded by architects and as a pillar of the reuse sector in Lyon, focused on collecting reusable materials.[^francenum-mineka]

**Interpretation:** Minéka is less “national digital marketplace” and more **local depot + redistribution + social economy**. It shows that the French bubble has multiple scales.

**Confidence:** high.

---

### 4.9 RAEDIFICARE — Southern France sector-building and platform work

**Role in the bubble:** RAEDIFICARE is a regional professional reuse-sector actor, especially relevant to Southern France and Toulouse-related work.

RAEDIFICARE states that it aims to initiate the professional sector for reuse of building materials and that it is supported by Région Sud and ADEME.[^raedificare] The RAEDIFICARE platform describes itself as specialized in the reuse of low-carbon materials from buildings and as connecting resellers and buyers for rapid reuse.[^raedificare-platform]

**Interpretation:** RAEDIFICARE is not only a reseller; it works as a **regional field-builder**.

**Confidence:** high for self-description; medium for independent assessment of impact.

---

### 4.10 RéaVie — social platform and reconditioning/redistribution node

**Role in the bubble:** RéaVie is part of the social and physical-material layer in Greater Paris / Île-de-France.

RéaVie states that it is part of an inclusive and global approach to transform practices in the building sector.[^reavie] Grand Paris Circulaire describes RéaVie as enabling reconditioning, sale of reclaimed materials and creation of furniture and second-work items in reuse or new construction materials through an R&D unit and European collaborations.[^gpc-reavie] Seine-Saint-Denis describes RéaVie as an environmental association that develops reuse of construction materials to reduce BTP waste and also trains people in professional integration, with platforms including La Courneuve.[^ssd-reavie]

**Interpretation:** RéaVie is relevant because it connects material reuse to employment/insertion and local reuse infrastructure.

**Confidence:** high for role; medium for scale.

---

### 4.11 Plateau Urbain + Bellastock — connecting vacant-space reuse and material reuse

**Role in the bubble:** this partnership connects two reuse questions: how to reuse spaces and how to reuse materials.

Plateau Urbain announced that it was teaming up with Bellastock to repurpose spaces and materials; the article says their objectives are to anticipate building transformation through temporary occupation, maximize reuse and reversibility in temporary installations, and pool expertise between transitional urbanism and circular economy.[^plateau-bellastock]

**Interpretation:** this is important because material reuse often happens too late, at demolition. Temporary occupation can reveal inventories, test demand and reduce unnecessary demolition.

**Confidence:** high for partnership statement; medium for implemented project outcomes.

## 5. Buildings, demonstrators and physical infrastructures

### 5.1 Actlab, Île-Saint-Denis

**Actors:** Bellastock, Plaine Commune, Plaine Commune Développement.

**Evidence:** Bellastock states that Actlab was the first manifesto laboratory for material reuse in France, co-constructed with Plaine Commune and Plaine Commune Développement and installed in 2012 at the Ecoquartier Fluvial construction site.[^actlab] Bellastock also says the Actlab material library was built from materials recovered from demolition of the Printemps warehouses, and that the Base-vie prototype was built with more than 70% reused materials.[^actlab]

**Knowledge value:** Actlab shows a construction site becoming a material library, public laboratory, prototyping workshop and training/cultural space. It is not just a building with reused materials; it is a **reuse learning infrastructure**.

**Confidence:** high.

---

### 5.2 La Fabrique du Clos, Stains

**Actors:** Bellastock, ANRU, Seine-Saint-Denis Habitat, Plaine Commune, Ville de Stains, local associations and insertion structures.

**Evidence:** Bellastock describes La Fabrique du Clos as an experimental chantier equipment project in the NPNRU context of Clos Saint-Lazare in Stains. It functioned as a recyclerie for storing demolition materials, a prototype site for reused materials for future public spaces and buildings, a convivial garden/stage, a training space with insertion companies, and a space of awareness and artistic experimentation.[^fabrique-clos]

Bellastock explicitly states that the project aimed at replicability at neighbourhood scale, to include components in future urban and architectural prescription documents.[^fabrique-clos]

**Knowledge value:** This is one of the strongest French examples of **territorial reuse infrastructure**: demolition material becomes a neighbourhood resource.

**Confidence:** high.

---

### 5.3 Maison des Canaux, Paris

**Actors:** City of Paris, Les Canaux, circular/social-economy companies.

**Evidence:** Paris.fr describes the Maison des Canaux renovation as circular and solidarity-based, with a terrace built with 90% reused materials including metal structure, and with 100% of interior insulation, wall finishes, floors and fittings such as partitions being second-life materials.[^maison-paris] Les Canaux states that its renovation followed circular-economy principles and mobilized more than 20 circular and solidarity structures.[^maison-canaux]

**Knowledge value:** Maison des Canaux is important as a **public-facing demonstrator**: it makes circular renovation legible to clients, visitors and policy actors.

**Confidence:** high for reported quantities; medium for independent verification of exact material percentages.

---

### 5.4 Pavillon Circulaire, Paris

**Actors:** Encore Heureux, Pavillon de l’Arsenal, City of Paris context.

**Evidence:** Encore Heureux documents multiple reclaimed material streams: façade made from 180 wooden doors from a housing-renovation operation, insulation from supermarket-roof works, timber structure from leftovers from a retirement-home construction site, floors/walls from former exhibition panels, and terrace grating from Paris Plage.[^pavillon-circulaire]

Pavillon de l’Arsenal describes the pavilion as designed for disassembly and reinstallation as a sports-club clubhouse in Paris’s 15th arrondissement.[^pavillon-arsenal]

**Knowledge value:** The pavilion is a public demonstrator of reuse culture: a compact, readable example where material origins are part of the architectural argument.

**Confidence:** high.

---

### 5.5 Pavillon Keller, Paris

**Actors:** Bellastock and project partners in the Beaugrenelle/Keller context.

**Evidence:** Bellastock describes Pavillon Keller as a restructuring project in Paris’s 15th arrondissement, with Bellastock supporting a global circular-economy strategy since 2021. The page states that nearly 70% of materials used are from reuse and/or bio-based/geosourced sources; it identifies in-situ reuse of guardrails, concrete terrace slabs, metalwork and false ceilings, plus incoming flows such as sanitary equipment, raised technical floors and ceiling coverings.[^pavillon-keller]

Bellastock also reports 69 tonnes of materials identified and 52.6 tonnes reused.[^pavillon-keller]

**Knowledge value:** Pavillon Keller is useful because it mixes material reuse with bio/geosourced material strategy and shows how contemporary projects often combine multiple “circular/low-carbon” pathways.

**Confidence:** high for Bellastock-reported figures; medium for independent verification.

---

### 5.6 Cycle Up Noisy-le-Sec workshop

**Actors:** Cycle Up, Région Île-de-France, Acorus/Point.P relationships in later reporting.

**Evidence:** Région Île-de-France describes a Noisy-le-Sec workshop for reconditioning reuse materials from regional construction sites, including reconditioning, upcycling and storage equipment, and an annual target of 6,000 sanitary fixtures.[^cycleup-idf] Ekopolis describes Cycle Up’s new Noisy-le-Sec site at 75 rue du Parc after a 2025 move, with reconditioning of ceramic sanitary equipment such as WCs, sinks, hand basins and shower trays, plus sorting, dismantling, cleaning and recomposition before conditioning.[^ekopolis-cycleup-2026]

**Knowledge value:** This is one of the best examples of reuse moving from “finding materials” to **industrialized reconditioning**.

**Confidence:** high for existence and described function; medium for achieved throughput.

### 5.7 Cycle Up / Eiffage / Demcy / ITEM workshop, Rillieux-la-Pape

**Actors:** Cycle Up, Eiffage Construction, Demcy, ITEM.

**Evidence:** Eiffage states that Cycle Up, Eiffage Construction, Demcy and ITEM created a 360 m² workshop in Rillieux-la-Pape to recondition finishing materials from deconstruction sites, initially ceramic sanitary ware and cable trays.[^cycleup-eiffage]

**Knowledge value:** This shows the French reuse bubble extending into **regional industrial partnerships** beyond Île-de-France.

**Confidence:** high.

### 5.8 Mobius Réemploi Bondy platform

**Actors:** Mobius Réemploi, Région Île-de-France.

**Evidence:** Région Île-de-France describes Mobius Réemploi’s new premises in Bondy, with equipment and machines to develop reconditioned raised-floor tiles, brick-reconditioning lines and furniture upcycling.[^mobius-idf]

**Knowledge value:** This is another sign that reuse is moving into specialized material streams and preparation lines.

**Confidence:** high for project description; medium for operational outputs.

---

### 5.9 LIFE Waste2Build pilot operations, Toulouse Métropole

**Actors:** Toulouse Métropole, Synethic, TBS Education, CSTB, FFB Haute-Garonne, INEC, Envirobat Occitanie, and regional construction actors.

**Evidence:** Envirobat Occitanie lists Toulouse Métropole and six partners and describes goals to reduce BTP impact by 20%, structure the circular BTP sector, implement more circular purchasing policies and build sector skills.[^life-envirobat] Toulouse Métropole says Life Waste2Build relies on public procurement to develop second-life materials and begins by inventorying available materials and classifying them by possible reuse.[^life-toulouse] CSTB says tools were developed for 58 pilot construction sites and mentions targets of 85% waste recycled by tonnes and 5% materials reused by financial value.[^life-cstb]

**Knowledge value:** LIFE Waste2Build is one of France’s clearest examples of **public procurement as reuse infrastructure**.

**Confidence:** high for programme design and targets; medium for realized outcomes across all sites.

## 6. Material-flow logic: how the French bubble works

```text
1. Existing building / renovation / demolition
      ↓
2. PEMD diagnostic or diagnostic ressources
      ↓
3. Material characterization
      ↓
4. Routing decision
      ├─ in-situ reuse
      ├─ ex-situ reuse through a receiving project
      ├─ platform listing / resale
      ├─ reconditioning workshop
      ├─ donation / ESS / local depot
      └─ recycling / recovery / disposal if reuse not feasible
      ↓
5. Supply visibility
      ├─ Cycle Up
      ├─ Backacia
      ├─ Opalis
      ├─ RAEDIFICARE platform
      ├─ Minéka / RéaVie / local platforms
      └─ Looping / demand tools
      ↓
6. Technical and insurance checks
      ├─ CSTB / SPIROU
      ├─ Qualiconsult
      ├─ Mobius / Cycle Up confidence levels
      └─ FCRBE insurance good-practice resources
      ↓
7. Procurement and prescription
      ├─ Booster du Réemploi / A4MT
      ├─ public clients
      ├─ project-owner requirements
      └─ tender documents and product-family methods
      ↓
8. Receiving building / project
      ↓
9. Feedback, case study, guide, material sheet
      ↓
10. Stronger reuse infrastructure for the next project
```

**Core insight:** the most infrastructural French cases are those that close loops between steps 2–9. A one-off project may reuse material; an infrastructural project produces information, protocols, supply pathways and demand signals that make the next reuse easier.

## 7. France as several connected regional bubbles

### 7.1 Île-de-France / Paris / Grand Paris

**Main actors:** Bellastock, Actlab, Fabrique du Clos, Cycle Up, Backacia, RéaVie, Mobius, Booster du Réemploi, CSTB/SPIROU, Opalis France, Métropole du Grand Paris, Paris/Les Canaux, Plateau Urbain.

**Why it matters:** densest cluster of large real-estate transformations, public clients, reuse platforms, research institutions and showcase projects.

### 7.2 Toulouse / Occitanie

**Main actors:** LIFE Waste2Build, Toulouse Métropole, Envirobat Occitanie, CSTB, Synethic, FFB 31, INEC, TBS Education, RAEDIFICARE platform.

**Why it matters:** strongest example of circular public procurement and territorial construction-material reuse.

### 7.3 Lyon / Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes

**Main actors:** Minéka, Cycle Up/Eiffage/Demcy/ITEM Rillieux-la-Pape, Villeurbanne/Lyon local reuse culture.

**Why it matters:** strong local-depot and reconditioning logic; less centralized than Paris but important for physical reuse infrastructure.

### 7.4 Southern France / Région Sud

**Main actors:** RAEDIFICARE, Région Sud, ADEME support.

**Why it matters:** professional-sector development and marketplace/platform logic beyond the Paris core.

### 7.5 Northwestern Europe connection

**Main actors:** Bellastock, Rotor, Opalis, FCRBE, PREUSE, Métropole du Grand Paris, Lorient, La Fabrique des Quartiers, LIST and others.

**Why it matters:** this is where France connects to Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and wider European reuse policy/research.

## 9. Evaluation: how to place the French bubble in your matrix

### 9.1 Reuse intensity

France has examples of **high reuse intensity** at project scale — Actlab, Fabrique du Clos, Maison des Canaux, Pavillon Circulaire, and Pavillon Keller — but the national system also includes mixed circularity, recycling and eco-design. Therefore, the French bubble should not be scored as “all projects are high-reuse.” Instead, it should be scored as **high reuse capacity** because it has many mechanisms that enable high reuse.

Suggested score: **4 / 5 for reuse capacity**, with variability by project.

### 9.2 Infrastructural potential

France scores very high on infrastructural potential because it has policy, technical assurance, public procurement, digital platforms, local depots, market actors, research and demonstrators.

Suggested score: **4.5 / 5 for infrastructural potential**.

### 9.3 The main weakness

The French ecosystem is distributed and partly fragmented. Many actors are regionally strong but not necessarily integrated into a single national operating system. Also, many sources report programme goals or self-described services; independent data on achieved tonnes, repeat use, avoided carbon, cost, insurance outcomes and long-term project replication is still uneven.

Suggested caveat: **high institutional and methodological infrastructure, medium evidence on realized national-scale material throughput**.

## 11. Evidence caveats

1. **Self-reporting remains a limitation.** Many project quantities come from actor pages. They are valid evidence of what the actor reports, but not always independently audited.
2. **Reuse and recycling are often mixed.** French policy and project sources frequently combine _réemploi_, _réutilisation_, _recyclage_, _valorisation_, bio-based materials and eco-design. This file privileges claims where actual reuse or second-life materials are stated.
3. **Programme goals are not outcomes.** LIFE Waste2Build, PREUSE, SPIROU and Booster du Réemploi have well-evidenced goals and structures. Their realized material impacts require separate project-by-project data.
4. **The ecosystem is polycentric.** Paris is the densest cluster, but important reuse work happens in Lyon, Toulouse, Southern France, Brittany and Hauts-de-France.
5. **The term “bubble” is interpretive.** The sources prove actors, tools, projects and relationships. The claim that they form a bubble is a synthesis based on observed overlap.

## 13. Short narrative for a presentation

France’s reclaimed-material reuse ecosystem is a **polycentric infrastructure** rather than a single platform. In Île-de-France, Bellastock built early cultural and technical legitimacy through Actlab, REPAR and territorial reuse experiments such as La Fabrique du Clos. Cycle Up, Backacia, Mobius, RéaVie and other actors create the market and physical logistics layer. CSTB, ADEME, SPIROU, Qualiconsult and FCRBE work on the technical and insurance problems that stop reuse from scaling. Booster du Réemploi and LIFE Waste2Build show a demand-side strategy: reuse can scale only when clients and public procurement create predictable demand. Opalis and PREUSE connect the French field to the wider Northwestern European reuse ecology around Rotor, Bellastock and public authorities.

The key point is that France’s reuse bubble is not only a collection of beautiful projects. It is a set of **repeatable conditions**: diagnostics, platforms, depots, technical protocols, procurement tools, reconditioning workshops, and demonstrators. That is what makes it infrastructural.
