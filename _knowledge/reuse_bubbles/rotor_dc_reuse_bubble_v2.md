# Rotor DC reuse bubble — evidence-backed network map

**Working note**  
**Date:** 2026-06-05  
**Scope:** Rotor DC as an infrastructural reuse actor in Brussels / Northwestern Europe.  
**Evidence rule:** every factual claim is tied to a numbered source note. Where a statement is an interpretation, it is explicitly labelled **Interpretation** and grounded in the cited evidence.

---

## 0. Core thesis

**Claim:** Rotor DC is not only a reclaimed-material shop. It is an infrastructural actor because it links donor buildings, selective dismantling, material treatment, storage, stock visibility, resale, delivery, documentation, design assistance, and wider European reuse-policy work.[^S01][^S02][^S03][^S04][^S07][^S11][^S12]

**Interpretation:** Its infrastructural value is not that every material it handles is scalable by itself. The value is that Rotor DC helps convert irregular demolition/refurbishment flows into materials that can be treated, described, priced, stored, searched, bought, and specified.[^S02][^S03][^S22]

```text
Donor building / surplus stock
→ sourcing and partnerships
→ selective dismantling / collection
→ cleaning, repair, rewiring, denailing, tile cleaning, preparation
→ inventory / description / pricing
→ storage and showroom
→ webshop / physical shop / consignment sale
→ reuse by architects, contractors, private buyers, cultural actors, public clients
→ feedback into Rotor research, Opalis, FCRBE, PREUSE, design assistance, training, policy tools
```

## 2. Internal organization: the reuse chain inside Rotor DC

Rotor DC’s own organizational description divides the work into four teams. This is important because it shows that reuse is not treated as a single design gesture but as an operational chain.[^S02]

| Team / internal actor | Evidence-backed role | Why it matters infrastructurally |
|---|---|---|
| **IN team** | Sources sustainable materials and establishes partnerships. | Creates the donor/supply-side interface. |
| **Process team** | Physically handles materials on site; restores, refurbishes, and describes them. | Converts irregular salvaged elements into usable stock. |
| **Shop team** | Handles marketing, customer service, storage, and delivery. | Makes reuse accessible as a market interface. |
| **Support team** | Manages accounting, HR, IT, and subsidies. | Keeps the cooperative and logistics functioning. |

Rotor DC also identifies specific skill areas: repair/transformation of lighting, ceramic-tile mortar removal, reprocessing of high-quality urban wood, furniture/hardware cleaning, and planning salvage operations in large buildings.[^S02]

## 7. FCRBE: European scaling layer

| Detail | Evidence-backed statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | FCRBE stands for **Facilitating the circulation of reclaimed building elements in Northwestern Europe**. | [^S11] |
| Lead | Rotor says it led the Interreg NWE FCRBE project from January 2019. | [^S11] |
| Problem statement | FCRBE states that only around **1%** of building elements in NW Europe are reused after first use. | [^S11] |
| Target | The project aimed to increase the amount of reclaimed building elements circulated in its territory by **+50% by 2032**. | [^S11] |

| Output: directory | FCRBE planned one online directory documenting more than **1,500** specialized reuse operators. | [^S11] |
| Output: audit | FCRBE planned a pre-demolition audit method for reusable elements. | [^S11] |
| Output: specifications | FCRBE planned four innovative specification methods for reclaimed products. | [^S11] |
| Output: pilots | FCRBE planned 36 pilot operations in large construction/deconstruction projects. | [^S11] |

## 12. Why this qualifies as “infrastructural reuse”

| Infrastructural criterion | Evidence in the Rotor DC bubble |
|---|---|
| **Recurring supply access** | Rotor DC works with its own dismantling, other suppliers, demolition contractors, real-estate companies, donor buildings, overstock, and deadstock.[^S01][^S02][^S14][^S15] |
| **Material-processing capacity** | Rotor DC and Opalis document cleaning, repair, rewiring, tile-mortar removal, sanitary-equipment revision, wood processing, cutting, and denailing.[^S02][^S22] |
| **Market visibility** | Rotor DC has a physical shop and online shop; Opalis creates a broader professional directory and technical documentation layer.[^S01][^S08][^S09][^S10] |
| **Storage and logistics** | Rotor DC’s shop team handles storage and delivery, and the Evere site is a large 14,000 m² physical platform.[^S02][^S04][^S05] |
| **Design integration** | Rotor design-assistance projects such as OXY, Multi, Recypark, Caserne Trésignies, and the Boardwalk show reuse entering tendering, design, testing, specification, and construction processes.[^S16][^S18][^S20][^S21][^S23] |
| **Public / policy scaling** | FCRBE and PREUSE create audit methods, specification methods, reuse centres, training, pilot operations, and public-authority frameworks.[^S10][^S11][^S12][^S13] |
| **Knowledge feedback** | Rotor’s Rotor DC project page explicitly describes knowledge exchange between Rotor and Rotor DC and says Rotor’s larger-scale research, assistance, training, and awareness work is meant to benefit organizations developing reuse value chains.[^S07] |

**Interpretation:** Rotor DC sits in the “system-building reuse infrastructure” category because it combines operational logistics with market access, design-assistance feedback, and European tool-building. The claim is not that Rotor DC alone solves reuse at scale; the claim is that Rotor DC is a central operational node inside a broader reuse infrastructure.[^S07][^S08][^S11][^S12][^S22]
